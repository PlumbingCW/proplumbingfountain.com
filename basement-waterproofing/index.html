Basement Waterproofing and Water Control in Fountain, CO

A damp basement can come from groundwater, surface runoff or a plumbing leak, and the right fix depends on which. We work on the plumbing side of the problem, which includes sump pits, interior drains, discharge lines and ruling out leaking pipes that look like seepage.

That scope is deliberate. Exterior waterproofing, such as excavating around a foundation to coat and drain the wall, is foundation contractor work. We say so when that is the real answer. What we can do is keep water out of the living space by moving it, and find out whether the source is the ground or your pipes.

For an active failure, see sump pump repair. For a suspected pipe leak, leak detection is the fastest way to know.

What we can do from the plumbing side

Our scope

  • Install or upgrade a sump pit and pump with a proper discharge.
  • Tie an interior perimeter drain into the pit where the home has or needs one.
  • Add a battery backup so the pump runs during power cuts.
  • Check for plumbing leaks that mimic seepage.
  • Install a backwater valve or check valve where a sewer backup is a risk.

We do not dig and coat foundations. If your problem calls for exterior work, we say so and you can get a foundation specialist's opinion.

Approaches compared

Interior drain and sump

Collects water at the footing inside the basement and pumps it out. Less disruptive than exterior work, and it works with a sump pump.

Exterior waterproofing

Excavates around the foundation, repairs and coats the wall and adds drainage. More invasive, and done by foundation contractors.

Grading and gutters

Directs roof and surface water away. Often the cheapest first step.

Dehumidification

Controls humidity but does not stop water entering.

These can be combined. A homeowner might fix the gutters, add a sump and still run a dehumidifier.

Radon and sump pits

According to the EPA, every home below the third floor should be tested for radon, and a sump pit can be a path for soil gas. A sealed lid on the pit helps. Test kits are inexpensive, and a result above the EPA's action level is a reason to talk to a qualified radon professional.
