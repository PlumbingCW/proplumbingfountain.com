Bathroom Sink Not Draining but Not Clogged: What Else It Could Be

When a bathroom sink drains slowly and the trap is clean, the problem is usually one of three things: the pop-up stopper assembly, a blocked vent or a partial clog farther down the branch line. Each has its own signs, and the order you check them matters.

By the Fountain Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

Start with the stopper. In many bathroom sinks, hair and soap wrap around the pivot rod and the stopper body, and they restrict flow even when the trap itself is clear. Pulling the stopper and cleaning it takes a few minutes.

If that does not help, listen for gurgling and watch other fixtures. Gurgling points to a vent problem. A slow tub or toilet nearby points to a line problem. This guide shows how to tell which.

Check the pop-up stopper first

Clean the stopper assembly

  1. Lift the stopper and see if it comes out. Many pull straight up, and some unscrew.
  2. If it is held by a pivot rod under the sink, loosen the retaining nut and slide the rod out. Put a bucket underneath.
  3. Wipe off hair, soap film and slime from the stopper and the drain opening.
  4. Reassemble, run hot water and watch how fast it drains.

If the stopper is intact and the sink still drains slowly, move on. If the stopper does not seal when closed, the pivot rod may be out of adjustment, which is a different problem.

Look at the trap and the tailpiece

You may have cleaned the trap and still have debris in the tailpiece, the straight pipe between the drain and the trap. Slime can coat the inside of a pipe and shrink the opening without forming a visible clog.

Take the trap off with a bucket underneath, look through both ends and run a flexible brush through them. Reassemble with hand-tight nuts and check for drips.

A blocked vent

A drain needs air behind the water to flow freely, the way a bottle empties better when you let air in. The vent pipe provides that air, usually through the roof. If the vent is partly blocked by a nest, leaves or ice, drains slow down and make gurgling sounds.

Vent problem signs

  • Gurgling from the sink or other drains when water runs.
  • Slow drainage across several fixtures at once.
  • Sewer smells that come and go.
  • Toilets that burp when a sink drains.

Checking a vent usually means getting on the roof, which is a job for someone equipped to do it safely. A plumber can clear a vent and diagnose the cause.

The branch line

If the stopper, trap and vent are fine, the clog may sit in the branch line that connects the sink to the main stack. Grease, soap scum and hair build up there. A hand auger can reach some of it, but pushing it the wrong way can make things worse.

What not to do

Avoid pouring chemical drain cleaners into a sink that is already standing full. They can sit in the bowl, splash and damage finishes and fittings. If you have used one, tell the plumber, since the chemical can harm skin and eyes.

Also avoid forcing a plunger on a sink with an overflow hole unless you plug the overflow, or you will push air and water out of it. A little patience saves a mess.

Overflow holes and aerators

Two small parts often get overlooked. The overflow channel inside the basin can fill with slime and give off smells, and a blocked aerator on the faucet makes the flow weak, which can make the drain seem slow. Clean both with a small brush and some vinegar-based cleaner that is safe for your finish.

Why the code requires traps and vents

Model plumbing codes such as the IPC and UPC require each fixture drain to have a trap and a vent. The trap holds a water seal that blocks sewer gas, and the vent lets the drain flow. When either part is restricted, a drain can run slowly even with no visible clog.

When to call

Call if more than one fixture is slow, if you hear gurgling, if water backs up or if you have cleaned the stopper and trap and nothing changed. Our drain cleaning page covers how we clear lines and what causes repeat clogs. A camera can show what is inside, see sewer inspection.

Fountain note: scale and the stopper

According to Fountain Utilities, hardness runs near 165 ppm in winter and near 350 ppm in summer. Mineral scale can crust the stopper, the pivot and the aerator, so a sink may drain slowly because the moving parts have stuck.

If you see white crust around the drain, a soak in a descaling product that is safe for your finish and a gentle brush often help.
