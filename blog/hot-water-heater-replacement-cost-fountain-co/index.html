Water Heater Replacement Cost in Fountain, CO: What Drives the Price

The price of replacing a water heater depends on a handful of things you can check yourself: the type of unit, the fuel, the size, how hard the install is and what the permit and inspection involve. This guide explains each one so you can read a quote and compare two of them fairly.

By the Fountain Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

We do not publish dollar figures here, because the right number depends on your house, and a number from a web page is a guess. What we do is state a clear price before work starts, after we have seen the existing heater and the space around it.

Use the sections below as a checklist when you ask for quotes. A low number that leaves out venting, permit or haul-away is not a lower price, it is a smaller scope.

The biggest factor: what you are installing

Types of water heaters
TypeHow it worksCost direction
Standard gas tankHeats stored water with a burnerOften the baseline for comparison
Standard electric tankHeats stored water with elementsSimple install if power is ready
Tankless gasHeats on demand with a burnerHigher unit cost, more installation work
Tankless electricHeats on demand with elementsMay need large electrical service
Heat pump tankMoves heat from the air into the tankNeeds space and airflow, check availability and local rules

Switching fuel or type adds work. Moving from a tank to a tankless unit can mean new gas lines, new venting, new water connections and sometimes an electrical upgrade. See tankless water heaters for what that involves.

Size and capacity

A bigger tank costs more, and a smaller one saves money up front but runs out of hot water sooner. Size depends on the number of people, the number of bathrooms and your habits. A heater that is too small wears out faster because it works harder.

We size the unit during the visit by asking how you use hot water. If you are replacing like for like, the old tank's label tells you the current capacity, and you can decide whether it has been right for your household.

Installation conditions

What the plumber looks at

Location
A heater in a garage is easier than one in a tight closet or an attic.
Venting
Gas units need a safe vent path. Changing or repairing it adds work.
Gas and electrical
Old lines may need updating to match the new unit.
Shutoffs and connections
Corroded valves and old flex lines are usually replaced.
Code items
A relief valve and discharge, expansion control and seismic or strapping rules where they apply.
Drain pan
Where a leak could cause damage, a pan with a drain may be needed.

These are the pieces that make one quote higher than another. Ask each plumber what is included.

Permit and inspection

A 2025 bulletin from the Colorado State Plumbing Board says water heater installation or replacement requires a permit and an inspection by the local authority, and must be done by a licensed plumber under a registered plumbing contractor. That cost belongs in a real quote, so ask whether it is included.

In Fountain, the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department handles permits at 719-327-2880. We explain the process on water heater installation.

Repair or replace

A repair is cheaper than a replacement, but it only makes sense if the tank is sound. A leaking tank body cannot be repaired. A failed element or thermostat usually can. If your heater is old, ask whether a repair buys enough time to justify the cost.

Signs a replacement makes more sense

  • The tank is leaking from the body or the base.
  • Rust is visible on the tank or in hot water.
  • Repairs have become frequent.
  • Sediment noises are constant and recovery is slow.

See water heater repair for what can be fixed and water heater replacement for how we handle swaps.

Efficiency rules that change the unit you buy

According to the U.S. Department of Energy, federal efficiency standards for water heaters changed in April 2015, and many electric tanks over 55 gallons now use heat pump technology. That is one reason the unit you buy today may not match the old one, and why size and price can differ.

What to ask in a quote

Questions that make quotes comparable

  1. What unit, what capacity and what warranty is included?
  2. Does the price include venting, gas or electrical work and new valves?
  3. Does it include the permit and inspection?
  4. Does it include removing and hauling away the old tank?
  5. What happens if the inspector requires a correction?

Insurance usually does not cover replacing a tank that simply wore out. It may cover damage from a sudden failure. Check your policy.

Fountain note: hard water and service life

Fountain Utilities publishes hardness of about 165 ppm in the cold months and about 350 ppm in the warm months. Scale builds on the bottom of a tank and on elements, and it can shorten a heater's life and cut efficiency.

If you are buying a new heater, ask about maintenance and about water treatment. See water softener and filtration for options.
