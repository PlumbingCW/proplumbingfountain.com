No Hot Water in Fountain, CO: What to Check Before You Call

If the water coming out of your tap is cold, start with the energy source. On an electric tank, check the breaker and the reset button. On a gas tank, check whether the pilot light is on. Those two checks solve a surprising share of no-hot-water problems, and neither needs tools.

By the Fountain Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

If those checks do not work, the cause is usually a failed heating element, a bad thermostat, a faulty thermocouple or a tank that has reached the end of its life. This guide walks through each one, tells you what is safe to try and shows you when it is time to call.

Hot water problems often surface on cold mornings, which is when demand is highest and old tanks give out. Knowing what to look at saves you from a cold shower and a long wait on the phone.

Start with the basics

Before anything else, confirm that the problem is the heater and not a single faucet. Run hot water at two or three fixtures. If only one is cold, the issue is that fixture's cartridge or valve, and faucet repair is the next step.

Check whether the hot water is lukewarm, not cold. Lukewarm water often points to a failed element in an electric tank or a thermostat set too low. Completely cold water points to no power, no gas or a tripped safety.

Electric water heaters

An electric tank has two heating elements, an upper and a lower, and each has its own thermostat. The tank also has a high-limit reset button, usually behind an access panel. A tripped reset button means the unit overheated, and it can trip again if the cause is not fixed.

What to check on an electric tank

  1. Look at the breaker for the water heater and reset it once if it has tripped.
  2. Switch the breaker off before you open any access panel.
  3. Press the red reset button if you see one, then restore power.
  4. If the breaker trips again, stop and call a plumber or electrician.

Handling wiring and elements takes care. A failed upper element makes the whole tank run cold. A failed lower element makes hot water run out sooner. Both are repairable, and water heater repair covers how we diagnose them.

Gas water heaters

Gas safety first

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before anything else.
  • Do not light a pilot if you smell gas or if the area is stuffy with fumes.
  • Do not use a flame, switch or phone near a suspected leak.

A gas tank usually stops making hot water because the pilot went out or the thermocouple failed. The thermocouple is the small sensor that tells the gas valve a flame is present. If it fails, the valve shuts the gas off by design.

Many tanks have lighting instructions printed on the unit. Follow them exactly. If the pilot will not stay lit after one or two tries, the thermocouple or the gas control valve is the likely culprit, and it is time to call.

Tankless water heaters

Tankless units fail differently. They show error codes, and the common causes are a blocked vent, low gas or water flow, a scaled heat exchanger or a failed sensor. Cold-water sandwich effects, where hot water briefly turns cold, are often a flow issue.

Check the display for a code and look it up in the manual. Many errors point to descaling or a vent problem. See tankless water heaters for what servicing involves.

When the tank is simply done

Tanks wear out. Corrosion eats through the steel, and sediment builds on the bottom. If your tank leaks from the body, repair does not help. A leaking tank needs replacing, and water heater replacement explains how we size and swap one.

Signs a tank is near the end

  • Rusty water from the hot tap only.
  • Popping or rumbling noises from sediment.
  • Moisture or rust at the base of the tank.
  • Hot water that runs out far sooner than it used to.
  • A tank that is much older than a typical service life.

A temperature setting worth knowing

According to the U.S. Department of Energy, 120 degrees Fahrenheit is a typical water heater setting, because hotter water raises scald risk. If you raise the setting to fix a lukewarm shower, think about who uses the water in your home, especially children and older adults.

When to call a plumber

Call if the breaker keeps tripping, the pilot will not stay lit, you see water at the tank, you smell gas or you are not comfortable opening an access panel. A water heater replacement also requires a permit and inspection in Colorado, which is another reason to bring in a licensed plumber.

Fountain note: seasonal hard water

Fountain Utilities lists its water hardness at about 165 ppm from November through April and about 350 ppm from May through October. Hard water leaves mineral scale on electric elements and on the bottom of a tank, and scale is a common reason a heater runs poorly.

If your heater is several years old and the water is lukewarm, ask about flushing the tank. If you want to treat the water, see water softener and filtration.
