Water Heater Installation Rules in Fountain, CO: Permits and Who May Do the Work

In Colorado, installing or replacing a water heater requires a permit and an inspection by the local authority. That is the plain statement in a 2025 bulletin from the Colorado State Plumbing Board, part of DORA. It also says the work must be done by a licensed plumber working under a registered plumbing contractor.

By the Fountain Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

For a home in Fountain, the local authority is the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department. You can reach it at 719-327-2880, and its office is at 2880 International Circle. The department can tell you what your specific job needs.

This guide sticks to what is documented. We do not quote code sections from memory, because they change, and your inspector has the final say on what applies at your address.

What the state bulletin says

The Colorado State Plumbing Board bulletin from 2025 makes three points. A water heater installation or replacement needs a permit. The local authority must inspect it. The work must be performed by a licensed plumber under a registered plumbing contractor.

That last point matters if you were thinking about doing it yourself or hiring a handyman. The bulletin is clear that this is licensed work, so confirm who is pulling the permit before you agree to anything.

The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department serves Fountain. Phone 719-327-2880, address 2880 International Circle. Call before the job to ask what documents they need, what inspections are required and how to schedule them.

What an installation usually involves

Without quoting code, we can describe the parts of a typical install, because a good plumber covers them. The old tank is drained and disconnected. The new tank is set, connected to supply and hot lines and fitted with valves. A relief valve and its discharge are set up. A gas tank needs a gas connection and venting, and an electric tank needs a proper power circuit.

Items an inspector may look at

  • Shutoff valves and connections.
  • The temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe.
  • Venting and combustion air on gas units.
  • Gas piping or electrical connections.
  • Whether the unit is secured and installed to the manufacturer's instructions.

Whether each item applies to your tank is a question for the inspector and the manual. See water heater installation for how we handle a job.

How to check a plumber's license

DORA runs an online lookup for plumbing licenses. Ask any plumber for the license name and number and check it. Ask who the registered plumbing contractor is and who will pull the permit.

Before you sign

  1. Ask for the plumber's license information and look it up with DORA.
  2. Ask who is pulling the permit and who will meet the inspector.
  3. Get a written price before work starts.
  4. Ask what happens if the inspection finds something to correct.

Questions to ask the building department

A short call to the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department saves surprises later. The people there deal with permits every day and can explain what your job needs. Have the address ready, and know the fuel, the size and the location of the heater.

A useful call list

  1. Ask whether a permit is required for the exact work you plan, such as a like-for-like swap or a change of fuel.
  2. Ask who must apply for it and what information they need.
  3. Ask how inspections are scheduled and what the inspector will check.
  4. Ask whether any adopted local amendments apply to your address.

Write down the answers and share them with your plumber. If a contractor tells you no permit is needed, ask them to explain why, and compare that with what the department says. The 2025 state bulletin is clear that installs and replacements need a permit and inspection.

What happens on inspection day

An inspector visits after the install and checks the work against the permit. The plumber should be there or available to answer questions. If the inspector finds something to correct, it is fixed and re-inspected. A good installer plans for that and tells you how it is handled.

Model codes behind the permit

The IPC and UPC are model plumbing codes, NFPA 54 is the National Fuel Gas Code and NFPA 70 is the National Electrical Code. Local authorities adopt and amend them, so the building department, not a web page, tells you which edition applies. We name them here so you recognize the terms when an inspector uses them.

Replacement versus repair

Repairs such as a new element or thermostat are different from a replacement. If you are unsure whether your job needs a permit, ask the building department. See water heater repair for what can be fixed and water heater replacement for when it is time for a new tank.
