Waterproofing a Basement From the Outside: What It Involves

Exterior waterproofing seals the outside of a basement wall so water never reaches the concrete. It means digging down to the footing, cleaning and repairing the wall, applying a membrane or coating, adding drainage and backfilling. It is serious work, and it is done by foundation or waterproofing contractors.

By the Fountain Pro Plumbing team · Published November 14, 2026

We are plumbers, so we say this plainly. We do not excavate and coat foundations. What we can do is handle the plumbing side of basement water: sump pits, interior drains and discharge lines, and checking whether a leaking pipe is the real source.

This guide explains how exterior work goes, how it compares with interior approaches and what to try first, so you can ask the right questions before you hire anyone.

What exterior waterproofing involves

Typical sequence

  1. Excavate along the foundation wall down to the footing.
  2. Clean the wall, repair cracks and fill voids.
  3. Apply a waterproof membrane or coating to the exterior face.
  4. Install a drain, often a perforated pipe in gravel at the footing, leading to a discharge point or sump.
  5. Add protective board and backfill with suitable material, and re-grade the surface.

The work disturbs landscaping, patios and sometimes utilities. It also takes planning around weather and access.

Exterior or interior

Exterior

Stops water at the outside of the wall. Invasive and costly in effort, and done by foundation specialists.

Interior drain and sump

Collects water at the footing inside and pumps it out. Less disruptive. This is the plumbing side.

Grading and gutters

Moves roof and surface water away. Often the cheapest first step.

Crack repair

Seals a specific crack. Useful for a single entry point.

Many homes combine approaches. Fix the gutters and grading, add an interior drain and sump and seal a crack. The right mix depends on where the water enters and why.

Try the cheap things first

Low-cost checks

  • Clean gutters and make sure downspouts discharge several feet from the wall.
  • Check the soil slopes away from the house.
  • Look for window wells that fill and drain poorly.
  • Look for plumbing leaks, such as a dripping pipe or a failed supply line.
  • Watch when water appears: after rain, after snowmelt or on a dry day.

A leak that shows up in dry weather points to a pipe, and leak detection will find it. Water after storms points to the ground.

What plumbers can do

We install and repair sump systems, interior drains and discharge lines, add battery backups and check for leaks. See sump pump installation and repair and basement water control.

If we think your foundation needs exterior work, we tell you, and you can get opinions from foundation contractors. Ask each one to explain what caused the water and why their method fits.

Signs the water is coming from the ground

It helps to know what you are dealing with before you spend money. Water that shows up after storms or snowmelt, at the same spot each time, and that arrives with dampness on the wall or a white powdery residue called efflorescence, usually comes from the ground side.

Take photos with dates each time you see water. They help a contractor understand the pattern, and they help your insurer if a claim ever applies. Most policies cover sudden events and not seepage over time.

Planning around the work

If you do decide on exterior work, plan for disruption. Patios, steps, landscaping and sometimes utilities in the dig zone are affected. Mark and locate buried lines before digging, and ask who will restore the surface afterward.

Get at least two written scopes from foundation specialists, and compare what each plans to do, not only the totals.

Flood insurance is a separate policy

According to FEMA, standard homeowners insurance does not cover flood damage, and flood coverage is a separate policy, including through the National Flood Insurance Program. Seepage and groundwater are also commonly excluded. Read your policy and ask your agent before you decide how much to spend on prevention.

Questions to ask any waterproofing contractor

Ask these

  • What is causing water to enter, and how do you know?
  • What exactly will you do, and what is included?
  • How will the drainage discharge, and where does the water go?
  • What is the warranty, who honors it and is it transferable?
  • Who handles permits and inspections?

Beware a pitch that promises to fix everything without explaining the cause.
