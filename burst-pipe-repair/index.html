Emergency Burst Pipe Repair in Fountain, CO

A burst pipe floods fast, so act first and call second. Shut off the main water valve, switch off power to wet areas if you can do so safely, and call our 24/7 line. Every minute the water runs adds to the damage, and the main valve is the one thing that stops it.

Call (719) 530-4349

Pipes burst because of freezing, corrosion, high pressure or a failed fitting. A burst from freezing usually shows after a thaw, when the ice that was holding the crack together melts. Cracked and split pipes show up as sprays, drips in the ceiling, or a sudden loss of water pressure.

This page explains the first steps, how we locate and repair the break, and how frozen pipes should be handled. For a hidden leak without a visible burst, leak detection is the better fit, and for a failed whole-house piping system see repiping.

A pipe has burst: do this now

In order

  1. Close the main water shutoff, usually near where the water line enters the house or at the meter. Turn it clockwise.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain remaining water, and one upstairs to release pressure.
  3. If water is near outlets, appliances or the electrical panel, switch off power only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  4. Move belongings away from the water, take photographs for your insurer, and call us.

We respond day and night, weekends and holidays. Tell the dispatcher where the water is coming from so we arrive with the right fittings.

Finding your main shutoff before you need it

Many homeowners have never touched the main valve, and an old valve may be stuck or leaking when finally turned. Find yours now, make sure everyone in the house knows where it is and operate it once. If it will not turn or does not stop the flow, tell us so we bring a replacement.

Some homes also have a shutoff at the meter, which belongs to the utility. Fountain Utilities can be reached at (719) 322-2000 if you need help with the meter side.

Frozen pipes: thaw safely, then check

If a pipe is frozen and not yet burst

  • Open the affected faucet so water can flow as the ice melts.
  • Warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels. Never use an open flame.
  • Check for leaks as the water starts to flow again, and shut off the main if you see any.
  • Call us if you cannot find the frozen spot, or the pipe is behind a wall.

Pipes in exterior walls, crawlspaces, garages and unheated basements are most at risk. Fountain sits at about 5,558 feet and cold snaps happen, so insulating exposed lines and disconnecting hoses before winter are sensible habits.

Callers often ask if a clamp is enough. A clamp is a temporary stop. For a lasting repair, the failed section is cut out and replaced.

After the repair: drying and documenting

Water that has soaked into drywall, insulation or flooring needs drying. We stop the flow and repair the pipe. A water damage company handles drying and restoration, and your insurer may have preferred vendors. Keep your photographs, the plumber's written notes and receipts for the claim.

Ask the plumber to note the cause, such as freeze, corrosion or a failed fitting. Insurers often treat sudden bursts differently from slow leaks.

Materials and certifications for repairs

Repairs should use pipe and fittings listed for drinking water. NSF/ANSI 61 is the common standard for components that touch potable water, and approved fittings are usually marked with it. A repair is not a way around code, and the work should meet the plumbing code in force locally.
