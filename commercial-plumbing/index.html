Emergency Commercial Plumbing in Fountain, CO

A burst line or backed-up floor drain can close a business for the day, and our commercial calls are answered 24/7. Close the building's main water valve, move inventory off the floor and tell the dispatcher whether the space is a shop, restaurant, office or tenant unit, so we send the right gear.

Call (719) 530-4349

Commercial plumbing differs from a home call in scale and in who must be told. Pipes are larger, drains carry more, and the building may have a landlord, tenants, a property manager and an insurer who all want to know what happened. We treat paperwork as part of the job.

This page covers emergency calls, grease and drain work, backflow testing, storm drains and property-manager support. If you are a homeowner, start with plumbing installation or drain cleaning.

After-hours emergencies in a commercial space

First steps for a business owner

  • Close the main water valve and shut off any appliance connected to the leaking line.
  • Move inventory, files and electronics off the floor.
  • Notify the landlord or property manager, who may have a shutoff you cannot reach.
  • Photograph the damage and the source for the insurer before cleanup.

We send a plumber after hours, on weekends and on holidays. A floor drain backing up in a kitchen, a burst line in a ceiling or a sewer backup in a shared restroom are typical calls.

Grease, drains and backflow: planned work that prevents calls

Restaurants and food service tenants need grease line and interceptor cleaning on a schedule, not after a backup. The right interval depends on volume and on what goes down the line. We help set one and keep a record you can show a property manager or an inspector.

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination. Testing rules depend on the water utility and the type of connection, so ask the utility what applies to your building. Fountain Utilities can be reached at (719) 322-2000.

Landlords, property managers and tenants

We receive calls from property managers on behalf of tenants, often with a work order number. Tell us who authorizes the work and who is billed, and we can confirm that before we arrive. A written summary of what we found and what we did goes to the person who approved the job.

A point on commercial leak calls

Commercial leak jobs often involve several parties and a long chain of approval. We are honest about what we can do quickly and what needs sign-off, and we tell you if a job is outside our scope.

Commercial codes and who to ask

Commercial buildings follow plumbing and building codes and accessibility standards that differ from homes. Requirements can include backflow protection, grease interceptors in food service and accessible fixtures. Ask the building department which rules apply to your occupancy, and we will tell you what we see on site and what to bring to that conversation.
