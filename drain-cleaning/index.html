When a clogged drain is an emergency

Treat a drain as an emergency when water is coming up out of a floor drain, a toilet or a tub that was not in use, because that means the main line is blocked and every fixture upstream is feeding it. The same goes for a blockage that has put standing water on a finished floor, or any backup that smells like sewage.

While you wait for us

  • Stop using sinks, showers, dishwashers and washing machines, since every gallon sent down the line ends up on the floor.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line. It sits in standing water and can burn skin and damage the pipe, and it makes the plumber's work riskier.
  • Keep children and pets away from sewage water and wear rubber gloves if you have to move belongings.
  • Photograph the water and the affected floor before you clean anything up, in case you file an insurance claim.

Snaking, cabling and what each tool can reach

A hand auger or drain snake is the right tool for a trap or a short branch. It bores a hole through the blockage so water can move again. For longer runs, a motorized cable with a cutting head is used. It travels farther down the line and can chew through roots and heavy buildup that a hand tool leaves behind.

Cabling is not a cure for a damaged pipe. If the cable keeps hitting the same spot, brings up pieces of clay or broken pipe, or the blockage returns within weeks, the line has a defect that cleaning alone will not solve. That is the point where a camera run shows the cause, and where sewer line repair becomes the real conversation.

Signs a clog is coming back for a reason

  • The same drain stops up more than twice in a few months.
  • The cable stops at the same distance each time.
  • You see roots, scale or pipe fragments on the cable when it is pulled out.
  • Water drains, but only after sitting for several minutes.

Kitchen lines, showers and everyday causes

Kitchen clogs usually come from cooking grease that cooled and hardened inside the line, coffee grounds, and food scraps that passed the disposal and settled in the first bend. Bathroom clogs are mostly hair and soap scum, and a tub drain often holds a dense plug just below the stopper. Neither is a sign you did anything wrong, only that the line has been in use.

If the kitchen sink and the disposal are stopping together, the disposal may be part of the problem, and our garbage disposal repair page covers that side of the job.

Fountain water and your drain lines

Fountain Utilities lists its water at about 165 ppm hardness from November through April and about 350 ppm from May through October. Water that hard leaves mineral film inside pipes and combines with soap into the scum that narrows bathroom lines. That is one reason tub and sink drains in this area can slow down more quickly in summer.

What the plumbing code says about drains

Model plumbing codes such as the International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) set rules for traps, vents, drain slope and cleanouts. That is why a drain needs both water and air to flow, and why a missing cleanout makes clearing a main line harder. States and cities adopt and amend those codes, so ask the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department which edition applies at your address.
