Emergency Faucet Repair in Fountain, CO

A faucet that will not shut off, or a supply valve that has started spraying, can empty a lot of water onto a cabinet floor, and we take these calls 24/7. Close the shutoff valve under the sink, or the main water valve if the shutoff is the leak, and keep towels ready until we arrive.

Most faucet problems are small ones that waste water or money: a drip from the spout, a loose handle, low flow from a clogged aerator, or a slow leak under the sink. A smaller number are emergencies: a corroded valve that snaps off, a flexible supply line that bursts, or a shower valve that cannot be turned off.

This page covers kitchen and bathroom faucets, tub and shower valves, and the small shutoff valves that serve them. Garbage disposal and sink drain work lives on its own page, and bigger leaks inside walls are covered by leak detection.

A valve will not close or a line is spraying

What to do before we arrive

  • Close the small valve under the sink, turning it clockwise.
  • If that valve is itself leaking or will not turn, close the main water valve for the house.
  • Open the faucet to drain the remaining water in the line.
  • Place a bucket and towels under the leak, and call us. Do not force a stuck valve, since it can break.

Corroded shutoff valves are the usual culprit when a valve fails. We replace them and add a quarter-turn shutoff so the next time it is simple.

A repair is usually best when the faucet is in good condition and parts are available. When the body is corroded or parts are discontinued, a replacement faucet is often the better value.

Shower and tub valves

A shower that drips, loses temperature control or keeps running after you shut it off usually needs a new cartridge. Some valves are reached from the front by removing the trim. Others need access from behind the wall, which can be through an access panel or a small opening in the drywall.

A tub spout that leaks around the wall may be a loose fitting, and a spout that sends water to the shower head when the tub is on may have a worn diverter. Both are repairable. Callers describe bathtub faucet leaks that start small and grow, so do not wait on them.

Kitchen faucets and the hard water problem

Fountain Utilities reports hardness around 165 ppm in winter and around 350 ppm in summer. Hard water scale clogs aerators and wears cartridge surfaces, and kitchen faucets with pull-down sprayers show it first. Soaking an aerator in vinegar often restores flow. If the flow is still low, the problem may be in the supply line or the valve.

Faucet cartridges wear out with use and with hard water, whatever the age of the house. If a faucet is slow to run even with a new cartridge, the supply line behind it may be restricted, and in that case see repiping.

Outdoor spigots and hose bibs

Outdoor faucets can split in a freeze if a hose is left attached. The damage shows when you use the spigot in spring and find water inside the wall. Disconnect hoses before cold weather, and call us if an outdoor faucet drips inside the wall after use.

Certification marks to look for

Faucets and fittings sold for drinking water are commonly certified to two standards, NSF/ANSI 61 for health effects and NSF/ANSI 372 for low lead content. When you replace a cartridge or a whole faucet, choose parts that carry those marks, and keep the model number for later repairs.
