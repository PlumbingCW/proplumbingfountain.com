Emergency Garbage Disposal Repair in Fountain, CO

A disposal that is leaking under the sink, or a kitchen sink that is backing up through a stopped disposal, needs help right away, and we answer 24/7. Turn the disposal off at the switch, shut the water under the sink if it is leaking, and never put your hand inside the unit.

A garbage disposal does a hard job in a small space. It chews food, passes it into a drain trap and relies on the kitchen drain line downstream to carry it away. When one part fails, the others show symptoms: humming without turning, a leak from the bottom, a dishwasher that will not drain, or a sink that gurgles.

This page covers jams, leaks, dead units and installation. For a sink that backs up even when the disposal is working, see drain cleaning, and for the faucet above it see faucet repair.

Leaking or jammed: what to do right now

Before anything else

  • Switch the disposal off at the wall switch and unplug it or turn off the breaker if it is hardwired.
  • Never put your hand into the disposal, even if it looks off.
  • If water is leaking, close the shutoff valves under the sink, or the main if those do not work.
  • Place a bucket under the unit and call us if the leak is from the body, since that means replacement.

If the unit hums but does not turn, a jam is likely. Many models have a hex socket underneath where a wrench can turn the grinder plate back and forth, and a reset button on the bottom. If neither helps, we can clear the jam or replace the unit.

Repair or replace

Usually repairable

Jams, a tripped reset, a loose drain connection and a leaking flange can be fixed without a new unit.

Replace instead when

The motor has failed, the housing leaks or the unit is old and repeatedly jams.

Sizing

Horsepower and feed type are a matter of use. We match the model to your household.

Electrical

Disposal wiring and switches must be correct, and we check them as part of installation.

New installs include a mounting assembly, a sink flange seal, a drain connection and a power connection. If the sink has no switch or outlet under it, we can quote that work.

What not to put down it

Items that cause trouble

  • Grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the drain line.
  • Fibrous food such as celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.
  • Coffee grounds in large amounts, which settle in the trap.
  • Bones, fruit pits and hard shells.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and become paste.

Running cold water during use and for a few seconds afterward helps clear the line. A disposal does not replace scraping plates into the trash.

Kitchen drains in Fountain homes

Fountain Utilities lists hardness around 165 ppm in winter and 350 ppm in summer, and hard water scale coats the inside of drain traps and pipes. Added to kitchen grease, it narrows the line and makes disposal-related clogs more likely. Where the kitchen line is short, the clog usually sits at the trap, and where it runs farther, a cable or camera finds it.

Electrical and plumbing rules around disposals

A disposal needs power and a drain. Wiring falls under electrical codes and standards such as the National Electrical Code, NFPA 70, and the drain connections fall under the plumbing code, which also addresses how a dishwasher drain connects. If a repair involves new wiring, have a licensed professional handle it.
