Emergency Hydro Jetting in Fountain, CO

When a snake punches a hole and the clog returns a few weeks later, hydro jetting is usually the next step, and we run it 24/7 when a line has stopped completely. Until we arrive, stop using water in the house and keep the cleanout area clear so the plumber can reach it fast.

Hydro jetting sends water at high pressure through a hose and a specialized nozzle. The jets cut the blockage and scour the pipe wall, so grease, scale and root hairs are flushed out instead of pushed aside. A cable opens a channel. A jetter washes the pipe.

The method suits some lines and not others, and a good plumber says so. This page covers what jetting does, when it is the wrong tool, and how it pairs with a camera look from our sewer inspection service. For simpler stoppages start with drain cleaning.

When jetting becomes an emergency job

Callers often describe a line that has been snaked before and now will not clear at all. A cable that gets stuck at about the same distance each visit points to a heavy blockage or a root mass, and an emergency jetting visit is a common answer when sewage is backing into a tub or floor drain.

Before the jetting crew arrives

  • Stop all water use, because each flush or load of laundry adds to the backup.
  • Clear a path to the cleanout and move stored items away from it.
  • Keep the cleanout cap on until the plumber is there, since removing it can release a flood of standing water.
  • Tell the dispatcher if a previous plumber's cable got stuck, since that shapes which nozzle we bring.

We take jetting calls at night and on weekends. If the line cannot be jetted safely, we say so on the phone or on arrival and recommend the right alternative.

Jetting is not a repair. It cleans a sound pipe thoroughly. If a camera shows a crack, offset or broken section, we explain that jetting would push water out of the defect, and the conversation moves to sewer line repair.

What happens during a jetting visit

Typical sequence

  1. We locate the cleanout, or an access point such as a pulled toilet if there is no cleanout.
  2. A camera look is often run first so we know the pipe material and the condition before applying pressure.
  3. The nozzle goes in on the hose, and the plumber works it forward and back, pulling debris toward the opening.
  4. We recheck with the camera, confirm flow with water, and show you the result before we pack up.

Pressure and nozzle choice depend on the pipe. Cast iron, clay and plastic lines each tolerate different settings, which is why a camera check comes before the work.

Roots, grease and scale in Fountain lines

Fountain Utilities lists local water at about 165 ppm hardness in winter and about 350 ppm in summer. Hard water leaves mineral scale on the inside of pipes over time, and that rough surface collects grease and soap. In cast iron drain lines it can narrow the bore noticeably, and jetting scours it away in a way a cable cannot.

Roots are a separate matter. They look for moisture and enter at joints and cracks, and a camera shows whether that is what you have. Restaurants and shops along Mesa Ridge Parkway and Fountain Mesa Road deal with grease more than roots, and our commercial plumbing page covers grease line work.

Cleanouts, codes and when jetting is allowed

Jetting is a cleaning method and not a code item, but the line it cleans is. Both the IPC and the UPC call for cleanouts so drain lines can be reached, and a buried or missing cleanout is a common reason a camera visit comes first. Which cleanout rules applied to your home depends on the code in force when it was built.
