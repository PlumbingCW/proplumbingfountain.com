Most leak calls do not start with the word leak. They start with a symptom: a water meter that keeps turning with every tap closed, a bill that climbed with no explanation, a warm patch on the floor, a stain that spreads on a ceiling, or the sound of running water at night. The job is to find the leak without opening more of the house than we have to.

This page explains how that works, from a simple meter test to listening gear and thermal imaging, and what to do with the result. A leaking water heater is covered on the water heater repair page and a burst line on burst pipe repair.

Is it leaking? The two-minute meter test

Check before you call

  1. Turn off every tap and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker, and make sure no one is running the washer or dishwasher.
  2. Find the water meter, usually in a box near the street, and look at the leak indicator or the dial.
  3. Note the reading, wait fifteen minutes without using water, and look again.
  4. If it moved, water is going somewhere. Shut the main valve at the house and see if the meter stops, which tells us whether the leak is before or after the valve.

That last step matters. A meter that keeps moving with the main valve closed points to the service line between the meter and the house, while a meter that stops points to leaks inside the home. Fountain Utilities can be reached at (719) 322-2000 with questions about your meter or your bill.

High bill, running meter and slab leaks

A high water bill is often the first sign of a leak that has been running for weeks. Toilets that run, irrigation lines, a pinhole in a copper pipe and a leaking service line all show up the same way on a bill. The meter test above is the quickest way to rule in or out a leak.

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe running beneath or within a concrete floor. Callers describe a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water with everything off, or water coming up along a wall. Slab leaks are located with acoustic gear and thermal imaging, and repaired by rerouting the line, spot repair or repiping, depending on its condition. See repiping if more than one leak has appeared.

Water service line, yard and meter box leaks

The water service line runs from the meter to the house. A leak there may show as a soft spot in the lawn, a pooled patch, or an unusual drop in water pressure. Callers sometimes suspect it because the meter keeps running with everything shut off inside. We pinpoint the leak with acoustic equipment and explain whether a spot repair or a line replacement makes sense.

Yard and irrigation leaks are separate. A broken sprinkler line or a failed valve can run for weeks and cost a lot on a bill, and we check the system as part of the job when it is the likely cause.

Ceiling, wall and crawlspace leaks

Signs of a leak you cannot see

  • A brown or yellow stain on a ceiling that grows after you use a bath or shower upstairs.
  • Drywall that is soft, bubbled or cool to the touch.
  • A musty smell in a closet, a basement corner or a crawlspace.
  • Baseboards that swell or flooring that cups.
  • Water noises when nothing is running.

These leaks can start at a supply fitting, a drain joint, a failed shower pan or a leaking appliance connection. Moisture follows gravity and can show up far from the source, so the stain is a clue to start from, not an address.
