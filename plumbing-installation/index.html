Plumbing installation covers new work: a bathroom added in a basement, a kitchen remodel, a laundry moved, a new fixture set, or plumbing for a build or an addition. It is planned work, and the details of the plan decide how smoothly it goes.

Planning an addition or a remodel

Plumbing is easiest to change while walls are open. If you are remodeling, bring the plumber in at the design stage, before framing is done. Fixture locations, drain slopes and venting all depend on the layout, and moving a toilet or a tub later is much harder.

Ask for the rough-in to be inspected before the walls close, and keep the permit records. A buyer or an insurer may ask for them later.

A sensible order of work

  1. Agree the layout and the fixtures you want.
  2. Confirm the permit path with the building department.
  3. Rough in the drains, vents and supply lines and have them inspected.
  4. Close the walls, set the fixtures and test everything.
  5. Keep the manuals and warranty cards in one place.

Fixtures arrive with their own instructions and rough-in dimensions, and those numbers set where the pipes go. Order them early so the plumber can use the real measurements.

If your project includes a new water heater or a softener, plan their space and drains now. See water heater installation and water softener and filtration.

The codes behind new plumbing work

New plumbing is built to the plumbing code the local authority has adopted, such as the IPC or the UPC, and the rough-in is normally inspected before the walls close. Fixtures carry their own standards, and their instructions list rough-in dimensions that the plumber works to.
