Whole-House Repiping in Fountain, CO

Repiping replaces the supply pipes that carry water through your house. It is a major job, and the honest first step is finding out whether you need it. One leak does not mean a full repipe, but repeated leaks, rusty water and falling pressure often do.

Call (719) 530-4349

We assess the pipes, explain the options and quote whole-house and partial repipes. Where the problem is a single failed section, burst pipe repair may be all that is needed.

This is planned work. You choose the timing, we plan access and protect your finishes, and we handle permits.

Signs the pipes are failing

Warning signs

  • More than one pinhole leak, especially in copper, within a short time.
  • Rusty or brown water from the hot or cold tap.
  • Low water pressure that has gotten worse over the years.
  • Pipes that are visibly corroded, green or flaking.
  • Galvanized steel pipes in a home built decades ago.
  • Water that tastes metallic.

Callers often ask whether to repipe after a second or third leak. A leak detection visit can show the condition of the rest of the system, see leak detection.

How a repipe is done

Process

  1. We survey the house, the existing pipes and the access points.
  2. We plan the new routes, protect floors and furniture and shut the water off for a scheduled window.
  3. New pipe is run through attics, crawlspaces and walls, with small access openings in drywall where needed.
  4. We pressure test the new system, reconnect fixtures and arrange the permit inspection.

A full repipe often takes a few days, and water is off for part of each. Partial repipes are quicker and are done when only one area has failing pipes.

Permits and inspection

Repiping normally involves a plumbing permit, and permit rules vary, so ask the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department, which serves Fountain, at 719-327-2880. Plumbing work in Colorado is done by licensed plumbers, and you can check any plumber on the state's DORA license search.

Preparing your home for a repipe

A repipe is easier when the house is ready. Clear access to walls, the water heater and under-sink areas, and plan for water to be off during parts of each day. Tell your household which fixtures will be out and when.

Before the crew arrives

  • Move furniture and wall hangings away from work areas.
  • Protect pets and children from open access holes.
  • Plan alternatives for cooking, drinking and bathing during shutoffs.
  • Ask who will patch drywall and who will paint.

Pipe standards and certifications

PEX tubing is made to ASTM F876 and copper water tube to ASTM B88, and components that touch drinking water should carry NSF/ANSI 61. The plumbing code in force locally decides which materials and joints are allowed, and the inspector checks the finished work.
