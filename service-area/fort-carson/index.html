On post or off post: who to call

On-post housing is handled through the Directorate of Public Works Housing Office and the housing provider, which the community calls Fort Carson Family Homes. Residents usually report repairs through their village Community Life NCO. If you live on post, start there, since those homes are not yours to repair.

The housing office also helps with finding homes off post. For houses and apartments outside the gate, in Fountain, Security-Widefield and the surrounding neighborhoods, the plumbing is the owner's responsibility or the landlord's. That is where we can help.

Moving in or out: plumbing checks

A military household changes homes often, and plumbing problems tend to surface at the start or the end of a tenancy. Before you sign for an off-post home, run every faucet, flush every toilet and look under sinks for stains. Check the water heater for rust at the base and ask how old it is.

A home inspector can look at plumbing, and a sewer camera can look at the line underneath. If a seller or landlord has a record of past repairs, ask to see it. Permits matter too. A 2025 state plumbing board bulletin says installing or replacing a water heater requires a permit and inspection by the local authority.

Water heaters and hard water near the post

Tank water heaters fail faster where water is hard, because minerals settle on the bottom and on electric elements. Early signs are popping or rumbling noises, rusty hot water and a tank that runs out sooner than it used to. A flush can help an aging unit, but a tank that leaks from the body needs replacing.

Gas units add a safety angle. A pilot that will not stay lit, a yellow flame or any gas smell means stop and call the gas utility. We cover repair in water heater repair and the swap itself in water heater replacement. Tankless models are a different install, see tankless water heaters.
