What the age of a neighborhood does and does not tell you

A house in this part of Fountain may be more than two decades old, but that alone says nothing about its pipes. Owners replace fixtures, water heaters and supply lines over the years, and some remodels reroute whole sections. We do not assume a material or a problem from the neighborhood name. We look at what is actually in the wall and under the slab.

Appliances are another matter. The U.S. Department of Energy says tank water heaters typically last 8 to 12 years. If your heater still has its original label from when the house was new, it is worth checking the date. Many have been replaced since, so look at the serial number and the tag rather than guessing.

Household checks that help

Five checks worth making this month

  • Find the main water shutoff and turn it to confirm it moves freely.
  • Look at the water heater for rust at the base, moisture around the fittings and the install date.
  • Open the cabinet under each sink and look for a damp floor or a musty smell.
  • Read the water meter, stop all use for twenty minutes and read it again. Movement suggests a leak.
  • Locate the sewer cleanout if the house has one and make sure the cap is in place.

None of these needs tools. If one turns up a problem, leak detection and water heater repair explain what we do next.
