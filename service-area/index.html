How coverage works

Fountain is our main service area, so most calls start there. Security-Widefield sits directly beside it and Fort Carson lies just west. Each town has its own page, with the water districts, ZIP codes and local points that matter for a repair.

If you live between these communities or a short distance outside them, call and give the address. We will tell you whether we can come and what to expect.

Fountain itself

Fountain was founded in 1859 as a railroad shipping center and has about 29,800 residents in the 2020 census. Fountain Creek flows south through the city, and newer neighborhoods such as Lorson Ranch and Mesa Ridge sit alongside older areas near Downtown Fountain. Water hardness runs about 165 ppm in winter and about 350 ppm in summer, per Fountain Utilities.

That hard water is why many of our Fountain calls involve water heaters and fixtures. See water heater repair and water softener and filtration.

Security-Widefield

Security-Widefield is an unincorporated community with 38,639 residents in the 2020 census. Fontaine Boulevard divides Security from Widefield, and Widefield borders Fountain. Water comes from local districts, including Security Water District and Widefield Water and Sanitation District, and the Security-Widefield page explains who to call for what.

Fort Carson

Fort Carson had 17,693 residents in the 2020 census and lies just west of Fountain. The Army established it in 1942 as Camp Carson and renamed it in 1954. On-post housing repairs go through the housing office, and we help with homes off post. The Fort Carson page spells out the difference.

Permits and inspections in the area

The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department serves Fountain and can be reached at 719-327-2880. According to a 2025 Colorado State Plumbing Board bulletin, water heater installation or replacement needs a permit and inspection by the local authority. Ask the department which office covers your address, since it can differ by community.

Fountain and the neighborhoods around it

Inside Fountain, newer subdivisions such as Lorson Ranch and Mesa Ridge sit alongside the older core near Downtown Fountain and City Hall Plaza. Shopping and business areas run along Mesa Ridge Parkway and Camden Boulevard, and Fountain Creek flows south through the city. We cover all of it.

Pikes Peak International Raceway and Fountain Business Park are part of the local landscape too, and commercial calls come from along those corridors. See commercial plumbing for what we handle for businesses.

When you are not sure we cover you

A quick way to check

  1. Find your town and ZIP code on the list above.
  2. If your address sits between two of these communities, call and give it to the dispatcher.
  3. If we can come, we tell you what to expect. If not, we say so right away.

What to expect when you call from outside Fountain

The dispatcher asks for the address, the problem and whether the water is stopped. For Security-Widefield and Fort Carson addresses, they will also ask for the nearest cross street, since neighborhoods and gates can be confusing from the road.

We state the price before work starts and answer questions about timing on the call. If your address is somewhere we do not cover, we say so quickly so you can call someone else without losing time.

Planned work across the area

Not every visit is an emergency. We schedule water heater swaps, repiping, softener installs and new fixtures in all three communities. Planned jobs follow the same rules as urgent ones: the price is stated first, the work is explained and permits are handled where they apply.

If you are planning a project, call and tell us the town. We will confirm coverage and talk through timing.
