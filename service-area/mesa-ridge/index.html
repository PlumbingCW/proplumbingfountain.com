Where Mesa Ridge and Cross Creek sit

Mesa Ridge is the name used for the area around Mesa Ridge Parkway, near Interstate 25. Listing sites place the residential streets near Mesa Ridge Parkway and Sneffel Street. Cross Creek, listed as Cross Creek at Mesa Ridge, is a development within the same area. Its 2003 service plan describes a district in the northern part of the City of Fountain that includes The Markets at Mesa Ridge shopping area.

The city's own economic development page lists Mesa Ridge as an infill and commercial area of about 367 acres, anchored by the interstate, US 85 and the parkway. That means a typical visit here mixes homes, apartments, shops and restaurants. The boundaries between the named developments are not published in the sources we found, so we describe the area by its anchor roads rather than by a line on a map.

What the planning documents say about housing

The Cross Creek service plan, dated July 2003, projected about 1,100 single-family homes, 225 townhomes and 500 apartments over roughly 407 acres, with commercial, office and open space land alongside. A plan is not a count of what was built, so treat those numbers as intentions. A 2012 Colorado Springs Gazette story also mentions Mesa Ridge Apartments on Mesa Ridge Parkway among the residential projects underway at the time.

What this tells a homeowner is limited but useful. The area was planned and developed in phases from the early 2000s, so houses, townhomes and apartments may differ in age and in who maintains the plumbing. We do not assume anything about pipe materials from the neighborhood name. The material in your own walls is something we confirm on site.

Who owns which pipe

Mesa Ridge and Cross Creek each have a metropolitan district, which is a local government unit that can build and maintain public improvements. The Mesa Ridge district's service plan lists water, wastewater, streets, drainage and parks among its possible services. The Cross Creek district's plan is narrower. It limits the district to parks, street landscaping, storm drainage and a non-potable irrigation system, and it says the district does not provide potable water or sanitary sewer.

Those documents describe what the districts may do, not who serves your address today. The quickest way to find your water and sewer provider is your monthly bill. If a leak or backup happens on the street side of your meter or cleanout, the utility or district is the one to call. If it happens inside the property line, the repair is generally the owner's. We can help you work out which it is on the phone.

Hard water and your fixtures

Fountain Utilities publishes hardness of about 165 ppm in winter and about 350 ppm in summer for its water. If your home is on that supply, scale can build up in water heaters, aerators and showerheads. If another provider serves your street, ask it for its figure.
