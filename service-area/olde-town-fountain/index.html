Olde Town is the city's historic central business district rather than a residential subdivision, so this page is written for businesses and building owners. It covers what the city says about the district, what to tell a plumber about an older building and where to read about the work. For service details, see commercial plumbing and drain cleaning.

What Olde Town is

The City of Fountain describes Olde Town as its historical central business district and as part of the US 85 Urban Renewal Area. The city's page names Ohio Avenue, Iowa Avenue, Illinois Avenue and Indiana Avenue among the streets involved in its plans, and it refers to a city map for the district boundary. We do not publish a boundary of our own.

The city's own history page says Fountain was founded in 1859, incorporated in 1903 and rebuilt after an 1888 train explosion. It also says the Fountain Trading Company building from 1898 still stands at 117 Main Street, and the city's Olde Town page dates Woodman Hall to 1905. These are landmarks, not a statement about any building's plumbing.

What the city is planning

The city lists a streetscape and public space program for Olde Town, including a feasibility study for a pedestrian greenway beside the railroad tracks and a wayfinding plan. It also lists drainage and public safety work at Woodman Hall, reuse planning for the Lorraine Center and an Olde Town Overlay District. Fountain is pursuing a Main Street designation from the state.

For a business owner, the practical point is that building work in a district under active planning may involve permits and design review. Ask the city before you start a remodel, and tell us early if your project touches walls or floors. The city's page does not mention water or sewer line replacement, so we do not describe any.

Telling a plumber about an older building

A building that has been remodeled over many decades can have fixtures and pipes from several eras in one place. We do not assume what is behind a wall from the building's age or location. What helps us is a short description when you call.

What to have ready

  • The address and which floor or suite is affected.
  • Whether you own the building or lease it, and who the property manager is.
  • Where the floor drains, cleanouts and shutoff valves are, if you know.
  • Whether the problem affects one fixture, a whole restroom or the whole building.
  • Any recent remodel or tenant change that moved plumbing.
