Ventana is a planned community with a public district behind it, so the first useful question after a plumbing failure is often whose pipe it is. This page explains how that works and what to check. For the work itself, see sewer line cleaning, drain cleaning and water heater repair.

What Ventana is

Ventana is a master-planned residential community in the City of Fountain. The Ventana Metropolitan District describes its location as generally at Old Pueblo Road and Hidden Prairie Parkway. Its amended service plan covers about 151 acres and plans for roughly 650 residential units and 18,000 square feet of retail space.

The district's original service plan was adopted in February 2006. The community's amenities, according to the plan, include a 13.1-acre park, a community center, a fitness center, a pool and about 12,000 linear feet of trails. Those are planned features, and the district's website is the place to confirm what is open today.

Water and wastewater here

The service plan says the City of Fountain supplies potable water to Ventana. It also says wastewater treatment is provided by an overlapping district and that the district's wastewater improvements are to be conveyed to the Fountain Sanitation District for ownership and maintenance. In plain terms, the water comes from the city and the sewer mains are meant to end up with the sanitation district.

That matters in an emergency. A blockage in the public sewer main is a utility matter. A blockage between your house and the main is on your side. Both look the same from a basement floor drain or a toilet that gurgles, so it helps to know the difference before you call anyone.

If a sewer backup happens

First steps

  • Stop using water in the house so you do not add to the backup.
  • Keep people and pets out of the wet area and switch off power to it only if you can do so safely and dry.
  • Check whether other houses on your street are affected. That points to the main rather than your line.
  • Photograph the damage and write down the time you noticed it.

The Fountain Sanitation District publishes a sewer and drain backup policy. It says claims are denied unless the district is proven negligent. If the cause is in the district's main line, it will pay up to a maximum of $1,500 toward immediate extraction and cleanup, but only with signed authorization.

The policy also says the district does not provide or arrange cleaning after a backup from an independent service line. The district's listed number is (719) 382-5303. Read the policy before you sign anything.

What we can help with

We cannot tell you which party is responsible for a backup, and we do not make that call for the district. What we can do is clear a line, run a camera to see whether the blockage is on your side and explain the result so you can show it to the district if needed. See sewer inspection and sewer line cleaning for how that works.

For ordinary calls, such as a slow drain, a leaking supply line or a water heater that stopped heating, the services pages cover what to expect. Fountain Utilities lists its water at about 165 ppm in winter and about 350 ppm in summer. If your home gets city water, that hardness is worth keeping in mind for water heaters and fixtures.

Check before you hire anyone

Plumbing work in Colorado is done by licensed plumbers, and you can check any plumber on the state's DORA license search. Ask who will do the work and whether they hold a license. Water heater work also needs a permit in the Pikes Peak region. The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department can be reached at 719-327-2880.
