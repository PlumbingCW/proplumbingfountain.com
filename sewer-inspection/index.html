Sewer Line Camera Inspection in Fountain, CO

A sewer camera inspection shows exactly what is happening inside your line, from the cleanout to the city connection. It is the right step after a repeat clog, before buying a home, and before any repair so the plan matches the pipe.

Call (719) 530-4349

A small waterproof camera on a flexible cable travels down the line and sends live video to a monitor. We point out roots, scale, cracks, offsets and low spots as they appear, and we can mark the location of any problem above ground. Buyers and sellers use it to avoid surprises, and homeowners use it to stop guessing.

This is a planned service, so it is scheduled instead of dispatched as an emergency, though if sewage is already backing up you want sewer line cleaning first. If the video shows a defect, our sewer line repair page explains the options.

When a camera inspection is worth doing

Good reasons to book one

  • A drain or the main line has clogged more than once.
  • You are buying an older home and want to know the line's condition.
  • You have trees near the line and want to check for roots.
  • A plumber cabled the line and the cable caught at a particular spot.
  • You smell sewage outside, or notice a wet or sunken patch of lawn.
  • You are planning a repair and need the camera footage to price it.

Many callers tell us they only want to know whether the line is going to cause a problem soon. A video answers that more reliably than a guess, and it is far easier to plan a repair than to react to a backup.

We provide the findings in plain language, show the footage and give you the footage to keep if you want it. A recording is useful for a seller negotiating with a buyer or for a homeowner comparing quotes.

Buying or selling a home in Fountain

Home buyers in Fountain often rely on a general home inspection that does not look inside the sewer line. A camera inspection adds that missing piece. For a house near Downtown Fountain, in the part of the city that dates back to the 1800s, it is especially useful because the line may be older than the house's finishes suggest.

For a house in a newer area such as Lorson Ranch or Mesa Ridge, the camera confirms the line is sound, checks the joints and makes sure no construction debris was left inside.

Ask for the footage

If you are the buyer, ask us for the recording and a short written note of what was found. It is a clean record to bring to the seller or to a repair quote.

How the visit works

Before, during and after

  1. We schedule a time and ask where the cleanout is and what prompted the inspection.
  2. The camera goes in through the cleanout. If none exists, we may remove a toilet to reach the line.
  3. We watch the monitor with you, pausing at anything that matters.
  4. We tell you what we saw, what we would do about it and what can wait.

The visit is usually short for a typical house, though a long line or a major blockage takes longer. If the line is blocked, we clear it first so the camera can pass.

How inspection findings are described

Municipal and commercial inspection programs often describe pipe defects using the NASSCO Pipeline Assessment Certification Program coding system. We describe findings in plain words so you can follow them, and a written summary is easier to share with a buyer, an insurer or a utility than a verbal description.
