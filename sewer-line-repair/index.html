Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Fountain, CO

A sewer line that has cracked or collapsed can send sewage into your home at any hour, and our dispatch line is open 24/7 for that reason. While you wait for us, stop using every water fixture, keep people away from affected floors, and do not try to dig or pour chemicals into the line.

Sewer repair begins with finding out what is wrong, not with the shovel. A camera shows a crack, a sag that holds water, a joint that roots have pried open or a section that has collapsed. Once the defect and its depth are known, you can choose between a small spot repair, a trenchless liner and a section replacement.

Cleaning alone only works when the pipe itself is sound, so we pair repair work with camera inspection and hydro jetting where they apply. If you are still deciding whether the problem is a clog or a defect, drain cleaning is the quicker first stop.

Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup is not the same as a slow drain. It means the line serving the whole house is not carrying waste away, and anything you add makes it worse. Tell the dispatcher what you see, since a toilet, tub and floor drain backing up at once point to the main line.

Until a plumber arrives

  • Stop running water, flushing toilets, and using the washer or dishwasher.
  • Leave the area, and keep children and pets out of standing sewage.
  • If the water is near outlets or appliances, turn off power to that part of the house only if you can reach the panel without stepping into the water.
  • Photograph damage for your insurer, and wait to clean up until the plumber has traced the cause.

Callers often say a previous plumber found roots and recommended a spot repair, which is a sensible path when the rest of the line looks healthy on camera. A camera run decides that, and it also tells us whether the line is clay, cast iron, or plastic.

Spot repair, trenchless lining or replacement

Spot repair

Best when the camera shows one defect in an otherwise sound line. We dig a small access, cut out the bad section and join new pipe.

Trenchless lining

A resin-saturated liner is pulled into the existing pipe and cured to form a new inner wall. It suits cracks and root-prone joints with less digging, if the pipe has not collapsed.

Pipe bursting

A new pipe is pulled through as the old one is broken apart. It is a trenchless option for failed lines in some conditions.

Open-cut replacement

Used when the line has collapsed, sags badly, or has been compromised over a long length.

We explain the trade-offs for your line. Trenchless methods can mean less lawn and driveway disturbance, but they need a pipe that can accept the liner, and in a few cases excavation is the honest answer.
