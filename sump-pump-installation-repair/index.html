Emergency Sump Pump Repair in Fountain, CO

A sump pump that has stopped during a storm, or a pit that is filling faster than the pump can empty it, is a real emergency, and our line is open 24/7. Move belongings off the basement floor, keep away from standing water near outlets and call us right away.

Call (719) 530-4349

A sump pump collects water from a pit in the lowest part of a basement or crawlspace and sends it out through a discharge pipe. When it fails, the pit overflows, and groundwater can reach the floor in hours. The common causes are a stuck float switch, a clogged intake, a failed motor, a power outage and a blocked or frozen discharge line.

This page explains how we get a failed pump running, when a replacement or a battery backup is the better choice and what a new installation includes. A sewage ejector pump, which lifts waste from a basement bathroom, is a different machine, and we handle those too. For water entering through the foundation, see basement water control.

Pit filling or pump not running: first steps

Right now

  • Look at the pit. If the water is rising and the pump is silent, check that it is plugged in and the breaker is on.
  • Lift the float gently to see if the pump starts. If it does, the float is stuck and needs attention.
  • Do not touch the pump or cord while standing in water. Turn off power to the area at the panel if you can reach it safely.
  • Move stored boxes, furniture and electronics up off the floor, then call us.

We respond to sump failures at any hour, including during storms. Tell the dispatcher if the discharge hose runs outside, since a frozen or blocked outlet is a common reason a pump runs but moves nothing.

Sump pumps have a finite life. An old pump is a risk even if it still runs, and replacing it before the wet season is cheaper than a flooded basement.

Pump types and backups

Submersible pump

Sits in the pit, runs quietly, handles most homes.

Pedestal pump

The motor is above the pit. It is easier to service and louder.

Battery backup

A second pump on a battery runs when power fails, which is when many basements flood.

Sewage ejector pump

Lifts waste from a basement bathroom or laundry to the sewer line. It needs a sealed pit.

A water-powered backup does not need a battery, but it depends on utility water pressure and uses water. We recommend what fits your home.

Does your Fountain home need one?

Fountain Creek runs south through the city, and USGS has published a report on the September 2013 flooding in the Fountain Creek basin, so water near the creek corridor is a recognized concern. Not every house has a sump pit. Many Fountain homes with basements do, and some do not need one at all. A pit is more likely where a lot is low or the water table is high.

Some homes have a sump pit and some do not, in newer subdivisions such as Lorson Ranch and Mesa Ridge and in the older core near Downtown Fountain alike. A basement that has stayed dry can still get water after a heavy rain. We look at your pit and discharge before advising.

Installing a new sump pump

What a new installation includes

  1. Locate the lowest point and decide where the pit will sit, or evaluate the existing one.
  2. Set the pit, connect the interior drain if there is one, and install the pump on a level base.
  3. Run the discharge line to a point away from the foundation, with a check valve.
  4. Test the pump with water, set the float and show you how to check it each season.

Where the discharge goes matters. It should carry water away from the house and not toward a neighbor's lot, and in freezing weather the outlet should not trap ice.

Standards for pumps and discharge

Sump and sewage pumps are commonly listed to UL 778, the safety standard for motor-operated water pumps. Where a discharge may go, and whether a sewage ejector needs a sealed, vented basin, are questions for the plumbing code and your local authority. Check the pump's label and the maker's manual before you buy.
