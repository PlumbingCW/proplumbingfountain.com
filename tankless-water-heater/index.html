Tankless Water Heater Service in Fountain, CO

A tankless water heater heats water as it flows, so there is no tank to run out and no standby loss. That makes it popular, but it also puts a lot of heat through a small heat exchanger, and in Fountain's hard water that matters. We install, repair and flush tankless units, including the descaling many owners put off.

Compare this with tank installation if you are choosing, and with water heater repair if you suspect an ordinary tank is the issue.

Descaling and flushing: the job most people skip

A tankless unit's heat exchanger has narrow passages. Mineral scale narrows them, makes the unit work harder and eventually triggers error codes or cold spots. A flush circulates a descaling solution through the unit, typically with a small pump and hoses, and clears that buildup.

Manufacturers publish their own intervals, and the right one depends on water hardness. Fountain Utilities lists about 165 ppm hardness in winter and about 350 ppm in summer, so follow the maker's guidance and lean toward more frequent flushing.

Many callers want to know what the flush involves

We connect a pump to the isolation valves, run the solution through for the time the manufacturer specifies, rinse the unit and check the inlet filter. Units without isolation valves can have them added at the same visit.

Condensate pumps and venting

High-efficiency condensing tankless units produce acidic condensate and need a drain. When the drain cannot run downhill, a small condensate pump lifts it away. If the pump fails or the line clogs, the unit may shut down on a fault. We replace pumps, clear drains and neutralize condensate where required.

Venting is the other half. Condensing units use plastic venting that must be sealed and sloped correctly, and a poorly installed vent can cause nuisance faults.

Replacing a tank with a tankless unit

What usually changes on a conversion

  • The gas line often needs to be larger to supply a higher burner rating.
  • Venting changes, often to plastic or stainless, and may route to a new exit point.
  • Electric tankless units may need heavy electrical service.
  • A water softener may be recommended to protect the heat exchanger.
  • A permit and inspection are required for the installation, per the State Plumbing Board.

A 2025 bulletin from the Colorado State Plumbing Board says water heater installations need permits and inspection and must be done by a licensed plumber under a registered contractor. The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department at 719-327-2880 serves Fountain. We handle the permit steps in our installs.

Standards, venting and the manual

Tankless units are listed by safety labs and must be installed to the manufacturer's instructions, including approved vent materials for condensing models. Gas piping falls under rules such as NFPA 54, and a tankless unit often needs a larger gas line than a tank. According to the U.S. Department of Energy, tankless heaters can last more than 20 years with proper maintenance.
