Emergency Toilet Repair in Fountain, CO

An overflowing toilet is one of the quickest ways to flood a bathroom, and our line answers 24/7. Lift the tank lid, push the flapper down or lift the float to stop the fill, then turn the angled supply valve behind the toilet clockwise to shut the water off. Do not flush again.

Most toilet calls come down to a short list: a clog, a fill or flush valve that has worn out, a leak at the base from a failed wax ring, or a cracked tank or bowl. A toilet that backs up along with a tub or floor drain is a different problem. That is a main line stoppage, and it belongs under sewer line cleaning.

This page covers emergency repairs, planned replacements and the small fixes that stop a running toilet from wasting water. For problems with the sink next to it, see faucet repair.

Overflow, base leak or spreading water: what to do first

Until we arrive

  • Shut the supply valve behind or beside the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • If the bowl is rising, lift the float or flapper inside the tank to stop the fill.
  • Stop using other fixtures if the toilet and a tub or floor drain are both affected, since that points to the main line.
  • Put towels around the base, move rugs and photograph the water if you may file a claim.

We take toilet emergencies at night and on weekends. If water is leaking at the base while the toilet is not in use, the wax seal or the flange is the likely cause.

A toilet that clogs often is a repair topic only until you know why. One partial clog is ordinary. Repeated stoppages may mean a venting problem or a line issue further down.

Wax rings, flanges and leaks at the base

The wax ring seals the toilet to the floor flange. When it fails, water seeps out at the base, often silently, and rots the subfloor. Callers describe a spongy spot in front of the toilet or a faint odor. Replacing the ring is quick if the flange is sound.

A flange that is cracked, corroded or sitting below the finished floor needs repair before a new seal will hold. Repair rings, brackets and new flange sections are options, and we choose according to what we find.

Replacing a toilet

When to replace

A cracked tank or bowl, a toilet that clogs constantly, or one that wastes water despite repairs.

Choosing a model

Height, bowl shape, flush mechanism and rough-in distance at the wall all matter.

Installation

We remove the old toilet, check and repair the flange, set a new wax seal and level and test the new one.

Haul-away

The old toilet leaves with us.

Replacing a toilet in the same location is a fixture swap, but if you are moving or adding a bathroom, a permit may apply. Ask the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department at 719-327-2880 when the plan is more than a swap.

Hard water and your toilet in Fountain

Fountain Utilities lists hardness near 165 ppm in winter and near 350 ppm in summer. Hard water leaves mineral deposits in the rim jets and around the fill valve and flapper seat. That can make flushes weak and flappers leak sooner than in soft water.

Mineral buildup from hard water can narrow the small passages in a toilet and show up as weak flushes. In a home near Downtown Fountain that has had the same toilet for a long time, replacement may make more sense than another repair, and a home in Lorson Ranch or Mesa Ridge can have the same buildup.

Flush volumes and efficiency labels

According to the EPA's WaterSense program, labeled toilets use 1.28 gallons per flush or less, and federal rules cap new toilets at 1.6 gallons. If your toilet is old, a replacement can use noticeably less water. Plumbing codes also govern how a toilet is set, sealed and vented.
