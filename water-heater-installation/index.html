Water Heater Installation in Fountain, CO

Installing a water heater in Fountain is a planned job, and doing it well is mostly about the parts around the tank: the gas line, the vent, the discharge pipe and the permit. We handle first-time installs, moves to a new location, upgrades to a larger tank and new construction.

This page is for the planned installs, where you have time to choose. If your heater has already failed and you need hot water today, use water heater replacement. If you are comparing a tank with a tankless unit, read tankless water heaters alongside this.

A good install starts with questions. Where is the heater going, what fuel is available, how many people use it and what does the building department require for the work? We answer these before we quote.

What goes into a proper installation

Parts of the job that matter as much as the tank

  • Correct sizing for the number of people and fixtures in the home.
  • Gas line size, shutoff and a sediment trap on gas heaters.
  • Venting that suits the fuel, the location and the appliance.
  • A temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe routed safely.
  • An expansion tank where the system needs one.
  • A drain pan and drain where a leak could damage flooring.
  • Permit, inspection and a final test of the new unit.

These details are why two installs of the same heater can differ in safety and in how long they last. We list what your install includes in the quote so nothing is a surprise.

Permit and licensed work in Fountain

A 2025 bulletin from the Colorado State Plumbing Board says water heater installation and replacement requires a permit and inspection by the authority having jurisdiction and must be done by a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor. The Pikes Peak Regional Building Department serves Fountain and can be reached at 719-327-2880.

If a contractor offers to skip the permit to save money, treat that as a warning. You carry the risk if the work is later found unpermitted.

Hard water and your new heater

Fountain Utilities lists hardness near 165 ppm in winter and near 350 ppm in summer. A new heater is the best time to plan for that, with a drain valve that is easy to reach, flush access and a habit of checking the anode rod. We can point out softener options without pushing one.

If your heater is in a newer house in Lorson Ranch or Mesa Ridge, ask what the builder installed and what the warranty covers. In an older home near Downtown Fountain, ask about the gas piping and venting that serve it, since those may predate the current heater.

Which codes govern an installation

A water heater install touches several rule sets. The IPC or UPC covers water piping, relief valves and discharge, NFPA 54 addresses gas piping and venting, the National Electrical Code, NFPA 70, covers power, and the manufacturer's instructions sit on top. The local authority decides which editions apply.
