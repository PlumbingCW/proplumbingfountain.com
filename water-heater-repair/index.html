Emergency Water Heater Repair in Fountain, CO

If your water heater is leaking or the house has no hot water, call our 24/7 line and we will send a plumber. While you wait, shut off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater, turn the gas valve to off or switch off the breaker, and keep people and pets away from any pooling water.

A water heater problem usually announces itself in one of four ways: no hot water, water that is only lukewarm, a puddle around the base, or odd sounds from the tank. Each has a short list of likely causes, and most of them can be repaired the same visit if the tank itself is sound.

This page covers the common failures on gas, electric and hybrid heaters, how we decide between repairing and replacing, and the safety steps for a gas smell. If the unit is old and the tank has rusted through, water heater replacement is the honest answer, and tankless service has its own page.

Start with safety: leaks, gas smell and scalding

If you smell gas near the water heater

  • Leave the building immediately and do not use light switches, phones or anything electrical inside.
  • Call your gas utility or 911 from outside, before you call a plumber.
  • Do not relight a pilot light or try to repair a gas heater that smells of gas.
  • Wait for the gas utility to say the home is safe before we start any repair.

For a water leak, the shutoff steps are simpler. Close the cold water inlet valve, switch off power to an electric unit at the breaker and turn a gas unit to off. If the leak is coming from the tank body instead of a fitting, the tank has probably failed. We arrive any hour and tell you straight whether it can be repaired.

Callers often tell us their heater was working at bedtime and cold by morning, which points to a pilot, element or thermostat. A gradual fade over weeks points to sediment. A fast diagnosis saves a lot of guessing about whether you need a part or a new tank.

Fountain water, hard water and your tank

Fountain Utilities lists its water at roughly 165 ppm hardness from November through April and roughly 350 ppm from May through October, with a recommended softener setting that doubles in summer. Water that hard drops minerals out when heated. That scale settles at the bottom of a tank and coats electric elements, and the effect shows up as noise, lost capacity and shorter element life.

Flushing the tank and checking the anode rod are two of the cheapest ways to slow that down. If the water hardness is a larger concern, the water softener and filtration page explains what a softener changes.

Repair or replace: how we decide

Usually repairable

Pilot and thermocouple, thermostats, elements, T and P valves, drain valves and fittings that leak at a connection.

Replace instead when

The tank body is leaking, the heater is well past its expected life and showing rust, or repairs would cost more than the unit is worth.

Ask about

Whether a repair really restores reliable service, since a repair on a failing tank may only buy a short window.

Permits and licensed work

The Colorado State Plumbing Board stated in a 2025 bulletin that water heater installation and replacement needs a permit and inspection by the local building authority, and must be done by a licensed plumber working for a registered plumbing contractor. In Fountain the building authority is the Pikes Peak Regional Building Department at 719-327-2880. Ask any contractor, including us, who pulls the permit.

Simple repairs such as an element or thermostat are repairs, not replacements. If you are unsure whether your job needs a permit, call the department.

Safety listings behind your heater

Gas storage water heaters are commonly certified to the ANSI Z21.10.1 standard, and temperature and pressure relief valves to the ANSI Z21.22 standard. The U.S. Department of Energy suggests 120 degrees Fahrenheit as a typical thermostat setting. Your tank's data plate lists its own certifications, and it is the first place to look before ordering parts.
