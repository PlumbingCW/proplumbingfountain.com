Water Softener and Filtration in Fountain, CO

Fountain Utilities publishes its hardness on the city water page: about 165 ppm, or 10 grains per gallon, from November through April, and about 350 ppm, or 20 grains per gallon, from May through October. That is a large seasonal swing, and a softener set for winter will under-treat summer water.

We install, service and repair water softeners and talk through whole-house filtration. A softener changes how water behaves in your pipes, heater and fixtures. A filter removes particles or specific contaminants. They are different tools, and the right choice depends on what you want to fix.

If hard water is wearing on your heater, see water heater repair and tankless water heaters. This is planned work, so it is scheduled and not dispatched as an emergency.

The city page notes that one grain per gallon equals 17.1 ppm and that these figures assume iron at 1 ppm or less. On common scales, water at 165 ppm counts as hard and water at 350 ppm counts as very hard.

That swing means a softener's regeneration settings matter. A unit set for 10 grains uses too little salt in summer, and one set for 20 wastes salt in winter. We help set a schedule that follows the season.

What hard water does to your plumbing

Effects you may notice

  • White scale on faucets, showerheads and glass.
  • Sediment in water heaters and scale on electric elements.
  • Scale buildup in tankless heat exchangers.
  • Soap that does not lather well and leaves a film.
  • Spots on dishes and glassware.
  • Narrower drain traps and pipes over time.

These effects are cumulative. A softener will not undo existing scale in your pipes, but it slows the buildup of new scale.

Softener or filter: which one

Water softener

Uses ion exchange to swap calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium. It reduces scale. It is not a drinking water filter.

Sediment filter

Catches particles such as sand and rust. Often installed ahead of other equipment.

Carbon filter

Reduces chlorine taste and odor and some organic compounds.

Reverse osmosis

A point-of-use system that treats drinking water at a tap. It can remove many dissolved substances.

If you are concerned about a specific contaminant, read your utility's annual water quality report and choose treatment that is certified for that contaminant. Fountain Utilities can be reached at (719) 322-2000 for its report.

Installing and servicing a softener

What a visit covers

  1. We check your hardness, the iron level if known and the household's water use.
  2. We size the softener for that demand and choose a location with a drain and power.
  3. We install a bypass valve, the brine tank connection and the drain line.
  4. We program the regeneration schedule, test the output and explain refills and upkeep.

For an existing unit that is not softening, common causes are an empty brine tank, a salt bridge, a clogged injector, a failed control valve and exhausted resin. We diagnose which.

Fountain homes and where the softener goes

A softener needs a spot with a drain and power, and a garage or basement utility area works well. A softener should not sit where it can freeze. In a newer home in Lorson Ranch or Mesa Ridge you may find a spot already prepared, and an older house near Downtown Fountain may need piping and a drain added. We look before we quote.

Fountain Utilities' 2024 report says its sources include surface water from the Fountain Valley Authority and the Southern Delivery System plus groundwater. Hardness can shift with the blend, which is another reason to check the city's published figures and not assume one number.

Standards for treatment equipment

NSF/ANSI 44 covers residential cation exchange water softeners, and NSF/ANSI 53 and 58 cover filters and reverse osmosis systems for specific contaminants. The U.S. Geological Survey classes water from 121 to 180 mg/L as hard and anything above 180 as very hard, which puts Fountain's winter and summer figures in those two bands.
